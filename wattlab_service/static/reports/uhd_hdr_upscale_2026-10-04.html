UHD, HDR and upscaling: where the watts go

One graded title (Netflix Meridian) delivered as HD/4K × SDR/HDR10 and as an upscale ladder, played on an OLED TV and seven streaming boxes. Wall power at 1 Hz on each device's own plug, three repeats per condition, 386 measured rows.

1 · HDR costs at the panel, through the picture mode

The C2 was fed by a Google TV Streamer at 2160p60. When an HDR10 signal arrives the TV leaves its SDR mode (OLED light 80) for its HDR mode (OLED light 100, contrast 100): +4.4 W like-for-like in FilmMaker, +3.7 W in the TV's own Standard modes. The box does the same work either way. Two of the boxes (Fire TV, Apple TV) are set to output everything, SDR included, as Dolby Vision; on SDR content that costs the panel +4.8 W for the same picture source.

2 · Upscaling: in the device, or in the network?

Every box draws more when it receives 4K made upstream than when it upscales the HD itself: decoding four times the pixels costs more than scaling. Whether the 4K was made by Lanczos or by an AI enhancement pipeline makes no difference at the device (≤ 0.1 W): it pays for pixels, not for how they were made.

6 · The network side

AI enhancement of the same Meridian sources on the OWL server (RTX 5080), measured in June: HD→4K 12.0 Wh per 45 s of content (≈ 0.96 kWh per content-hour, at 0.42× real time); SD→4K 14.6 Wh per 45 s (≈ 1.17 kWh per content-hour, 0.30× real time). A plain Lanczos upscale plus GPU encode of the SD rung costs about 0.6 Wh per 45 s. These are paid once per title; the device-side difference in §2 is paid by every screen for every hour watched. Per million viewer-hours: ≈ 1 kWh at the server against 300–500 kWh across mainstream boxes, before counting the ~4× bits the network must carry.

Method and limits

Content: Netflix Meridian (Open Content). HDR set: one P3-PQ graded source → HDR10 and a hable-tone-mapped SDR twin, same encoder (x265 Main10) at HD and 4K. Upscale ladder: SD/HD/4K rungs of the clean Meridian cut, all HEVC Main10, 4K rungs at 35 Mb/s. Each row: home-screen baseline, playback start, 12 s settle, 120–180 s window; presented frame rate checked on Android boxes (62.5 fps for 60p), content position on the Roku, delivered bitrate on the Apple TV. Rows where playback paused or the source died were discarded and re-measured.

Limits: one OLED model; one title; device level only (network, CDN and home router not measured); quality of the TV's own upscaler vs the AI pipeline not scored here (energy only). Apple TV numbers are VLC for tvOS, device-total; its HD rows were unstable and are excluded from the ranges above. The Xiaomi Gen 2 ran on a 1080p HDMI dummy, so its 4K rows are downscales. Boxes other than the Google TV, Fire TV and Apple TV ran without a screen (HDMI dummy or an undisplayed TV input).
